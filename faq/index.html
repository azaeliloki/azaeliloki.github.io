Certification

Certification, legalisation & acceptance

What the sworn stamp is, when legalisation is needed on top of it and which languages can be sworn.

A sworn translation carries my signature and the stamp of a translator accredited by the Cour d’appel de Lomé, which is what most embassies, universities and public authorities ask for. Some authorities also require legalisation on top of the sworn stamp. Togo is not yet a party to the Hague Apostille Convention: today the route runs through the town hall (mairie) where I am registered, then the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then the consulate of the destination country. Parliament authorised accession in August 2026, but the instrument of accession has not yet been deposited, and the convention will apply in Togo only after a six-month objection period, so not before 2027. If your file needs that legalisation, I can handle it for you. The administrative and travel costs remain yours and are stated in the quote. Ask the receiving authority exactly what it requires and tell me — I will adapt the form of the translation accordingly.

I am sworn before the Cour d’appel de Lomé, and every translation carries the official stamp and my signature, in the format embassies, consulates, universities and administrations expect. The decision on any given file always rests with the receiving authority — but if a document comes back over a stamp, layout or certification fault that is mine, I redo it at no charge.

Yes — every certified translation includes both a secure digital PDF with official stamp and a physical certified print. The physical copy can be collected in person in Lomé or sent by courier on request. For clients outside Lomé or Togo, digital delivery is standard with physical copies available on request.

Not in the sense of a sworn translation: there is no sworn status for national languages in Togo. I provide a signed certificate of accuracy, which engages my professional responsibility for the rendering.

French ↔ English (FR↔EN) only. This exclusive focus ensures the highest level of precision and domain expertise in both languages. For other language pairs, I can recommend trusted colleagues in the professional translation network.

Working Together

Sending files, formats & confidentiality

How to get a document to me, which formats are accepted and how your files are handled.

Send your document by WhatsApp (+228 79 71 62 58) or by email (contact@azaeliloki.com), whichever you prefer: both reach me. Accepted formats: PDF, JPG, PNG, Word (.docx) or any scanned image. The resolution should be clear enough to read all text. You do not need to send the original document — a clear photo or scan is sufficient for certified translations.

For source documents: PDF, JPG, PNG, TIFF, Word (.docx), Excel (.xlsx), PowerPoint (.pptx) and scanned images. For delivery: certified translations are always delivered as PDF with official stamp. The original Word file can also be provided for general translations on request.

Only the register of certified translations — number, date, document type and language pair — is kept indefinitely: it is what lets a receiving authority verify where a translation came from, and it holds no document content.
