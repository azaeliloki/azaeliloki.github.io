Transcription and Translation of Audio & Video Recordings

A recording is worth nothing until it can be read.

A statement, a field interview, a meeting, a call, a testimony, a piece of audio evidence — in a national language, in English or in French: while the content sits in an audio file it cannot be filed, quoted or challenged. I transcribe your recordings and render them in French or English, with time codes, identified speakers and every inaudible passage marked.

Scope

What this covers

  • Statements and interviews intended for a police station or a court
  • Field interviews and evaluation missions
  • Meetings, general assemblies, board sessions
  • Audio and video intended for publication or subtitling
  • Recordings produced in support of a dispute
  • Personal recordings to be filed — inheritance, family dispute, administrative procedure
Framework

Two regimes, and the difference matters

French ↔ English. I am sworn before the Cour d’appel de Lomé for this pair. The transcript can carry my stamp and signature, and the legal weight attached to a sworn translation.

National languages — Ewe, Kabiyè, Tem. There is no register of translators for these languages in Togo. The transcript is therefore delivered with a signed certificate of accuracy on my letterhead: I certify that the rendering is faithful and complete within the limits of what is audible, and that inaudible passages are marked and time-coded. My standing as a sworn translator appears there as professional identity, not as the basis of the certification.

On requisition by a judicial police officer or on appointment by a court, the assignment is carried out within the framework set by the appointing authority.

Transparency

Why national languages cost more

For French and English I work with a translation memory, a terminology glossary and centuries of settled legal vocabulary. For Ewe none of that exists: no tool, no corpus, no legal dictionary, no reference text to check against.

Three further difficulties are specific to speech in a national language. Spelling is not standardised, which commits you to a graphic choice held across the whole document. Speakers switch constantly between the national language and French. And administrative terminology often has no equivalent: it is no longer a matter of translating a term but of rendering a concept.

The working time per recorded minute is roughly double. The rate reflects it.

Method

What makes a transcript hold up

  • Time codes at regular intervals and at every change of speaker
  • Speakers identified and named consistently throughout
  • Inaudible passages marked — [inaudible 04:12] — and never guessed. On a procedural document a wrong guess does more damage than a gap
  • Sound quality stated in the document itself
  • Source file traced — name, length, format and date of receipt recorded at the head of the transcript
  • No summary, no rewording — what is said is rendered, including hesitations and repetitions where they carry meaning
Process

How it works

  1. You send the file by WhatsApp or email. Any format.
  2. I listen for two to three minutes to grade the audio and estimate the real speaking time.
  3. You receive the quote within 2 working hours: applicable rate, length retained, turnaround, total. No quote is issued without listening first.
  4. Production after you confirm.
  5. Delivery in Word and PDF, signed paper copy on request.
