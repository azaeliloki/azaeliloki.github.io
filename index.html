The Corridor

West Africa runs on two languages

A contract drafted in Accra is signed in Lomé. A Lagos judgment is produced before a Togolese court. A Dakar tender is filed in French. Every one of those crossings needs a sworn translation — and a document has no effect before the authority receiving it unless it carries the stamp of a translator registered with a court in that system.

That is what an agency in London or Toronto cannot supply, however good its linguists. I am sworn before the Cour d’appel de Lomé, and my stamp is what gives your document standing here.
